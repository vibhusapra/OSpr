Research + implementation / October 22, 2026

A voice tool that belongs on your Mac.

OSpr v0 combines native dictation workflows with accelerated local speech models. The goal is ambitious: match the useful capabilities of the best tools, then win on accuracy, latency, privacy, reliability, and design.

What to build around

Parakeet for fast English/European dictation; native-layout Cohere 8-bit for stronger measured English quality; Whisper and Qwen for broader coverage and language tradeoffs. Keep a strict raw transcript, deterministic vocabulary, reliable insertion and an optional refiner.

This is an inference from OSpr’s measurements and the models’ documented language coverage, not a universal winner declaration.

Where the product competition moved

Many competitors now bundle meetings, agents, mobile, sync and organization controls. Free local dictation and BYOK are already common. A generic “Whisper plus a hotkey” is not enough differentiation.

OSpr needs a better complete writing loop: fast start, dependable end, faithful edits, recoverable failures and no surprises about data routing.

Long-file completeness: finding and fixing a real truncation failure.

The old Qwen route shared an 8,192-token ceiling across a file. On the hour-long fixture it returned 6,734 words against 9,032 reference words, with 26.85% WER. OSpr now cuts long Qwen input at low-energy boundaries, at most 30 seconds each, and resets a 2,048-token budget per chunk. All samples remain contiguous; no recognized words are deduplicated. Reaching a chunk budget fails rather than saving a partial transcript. Chunk times are coarse boundaries, not word alignment.

Privacy boundary

Default ASR and text rules are local. Local MLX rewriting remains local. BYOK audio and text routes are separate, visible settings; provider keys go to Keychain. HTTPS is required except loopback, redirects are rejected, and provider error bodies are not surfaced.

Current source uses an owner-only paged SQLite history archive in Application Support, with verified migration from legacy JSON. The older normal preview still uses JSON. Capture uses private temporary audio. Completed takes are copied to an owner-only recovery slot before inference and deleted after successful transcription and requested history persistence or explicit discard. Optional review retention stores normalized audio separately under the history and storage-budget policy. Failed takes survive restart until retried/exported/discarded. No analytics or background content collection is included.

No claim of application-level history encryption, enterprise compliance, or complete network sandboxing. FileVault and the chosen provider’s policies remain separate.

Recovery and maintainability

The native app, CLI, MCP adapter and benchmark harness share the engine contract. Request IDs prevent response mixups; generations fence stale output; timeouts and cancellation terminate the owned worker. Raw and edited transcripts remain distinct.

Weights and package versions are pinned. The local process is replaceable with a Swift MLX/Core ML implementation after equivalent tests. No local inference HTTP service or persistent network credential is required.

The small development build uses its checkout. An optional self-contained bundle passed moved-path/offline smoke tests for all six engines. Signing/notarization and update recovery remain release work. Completed takes now survive inference failure/restart in a private recovery slot; native empty-result retry/restart/export and canceling discard were exercised; device-loss and permanent discard remain.

Close the release gates, then customize

A path from credible v0 to a leading tool.

Release acceptance

  1. Complete reference-speech microphone/PTT, device switching, interruption and HUD acceptance after the brief capture/recovery checks.
  2. Exercise insertion in TextEdit, a browser, a code editor, secure fields and a moving cursor; verify clipboard restoration.
  3. Validate the implemented native incremental stream, speaker-labeled imports, dual-track desktop capture and local excerpt notes before claiming interaction parity.
  4. Validate the bundled app on a clean Mac; sign/notarize and exercise install/uninstall, upgrade and login item behavior.
  5. Replay identical tasks in actual competing apps and publish raw, versioned measurements with correction effort and energy.

Training and model surgery

  1. Build an opt-in, consented dictation corpus: Indian/other accents, code-switching, names, paths, numbers, disfluencies, noise, silence and short turns.
  2. Split by speaker/session/device; isolate a sealed test set. Keep public benchmark audio out of fine-tuning and model selection.
  3. Try vocabulary/context biasing and calibration before training. Compare LoRA and targeted decoder/encoder changes against the same fidelity gates.
  4. Distill a fast streaming student from stronger ASR teachers with explicit error review. Preserve timing, corrections, punctuation and abstention examples.
  5. Only then prune heads/channels, quantize sensitive layers separately, distill the refiner and profile Metal/ANE kernels. Keep licenses and model/data provenance in the release.
Proposed winning axis: a native, fast, trustworthy writing loop with open measurements and no account requirement. Usefulness and reliability should be obvious in a five-minute trial; model novelty alone is not the product.
